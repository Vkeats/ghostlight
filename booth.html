booth

Pass ACX the first time.

A narration studio that reads along while you record, finds every misread, repairs the line, and masters to ACX spec — on your own machine, with nothing leaving the room.

Sixty seconds, start to finish

Recorded from the running app. The 99.3% is a real transcription score, the flagged misread is one booth found, and the three ACX checks are measurements of the file it produced. Nothing in it is mocked up.

Why this exists

ACX rejects a book for three numbers: RMS between −23 and −18 dB, peaks no louder than −3 dB, and a noise floor under −60 dB. You find out days after you upload, and the fix is usually re-recording.

It reads along

Words tint green a second behind you as they're confirmed. Anything you misread lands in the gutter with a timestamp — never inline, so the line you're about to read stays clean.

Fix one line, not the chapter

Punch-in: re-read the sentence and booth splices it back where it belongs, matched on word anchors and cut at silences.

It checks before ACX does

The same measurements ACX runs, on your file, before you upload. Including the one most tools miss — mastering lifts your room floor along with your voice.

Nothing leaves the room

Transcription runs on your machine. No account, no upload, no cloud. Your unpublished manuscript stays yours.

It knows the names

An invented name reads back as a misread in every chapter it appears in. Teach it once and chapter twenty agrees with chapter three.

Private beta

booth is in beta with a small group of narrators, macOS first. Builds go out once they're signed and notarized, so it opens like any other app — no security warnings to click through. Windows follows.
